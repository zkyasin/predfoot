Where every player stands, read from a TV picture, and how far off it could be.

predfoot recovers the broadcast camera from the pitch markings, places each player on the 105 × 68 m pitch, and gives every position an error zone built to hold the true spot 9 times in 10.

Top: public footage (SoccerTrack v2), with the pitch lines redrawn from the camera predfoot recovered. Bottom: the same players on the pitch, each with its error zone, and the area the camera sees. This only illustrates the output: the accuracy figures below come from SoccerNet test matches, whose images cannot be shown here.

Why it matters

Much of football's tracking data now comes from ordinary TV broadcasts. To turn a picture into positions, software first has to work out where the camera is pointing, and when that estimate wobbles, every player on screen moves with it.

The wobble inflates stats such as distance covered and high-speed running, and the positions rarely say how far off they might be.

Smoothing the camera

A broadcast camera turns around an almost fixed point, so its view changes smoothly. Worked out frame by frame, the camera estimate jitters, and every position jitters with it.

How it works

  1. 1 Find the pitch markings

    A neural network (PnLCalib, public weights, not retrained) spots the lines of the pitch, and the points where they cross, in each frame.

  2. 2 Recover the camera

    The pitch is a known 105 × 68 m shape, so those markings reveal where the camera is and where it points. This is called calibration; predfoot also measures how sure it can be of it.

  3. 3 Smooth over time

    A TV camera pans, tilts and zooms around an almost fixed point. predfoot uses this to smooth the camera across the clip and to catch frames that do not fit.

  4. 4 Place each player, with an error zone

    Each player's feet are projected onto the pitch with a zone built to hold the true spot 9 times in 10, tuned on past matches so it keeps that promise. A health score flags frames whose camera looks wrong.

Limits

  • Tested on three matches from one league and one broadcast style.
  • Players come from the dataset's annotated boxes: detecting and tracking them is not part of the project.
  • The alarm and the physical-stat measurements run in the evaluation scripts, not yet in the command-line tool.
